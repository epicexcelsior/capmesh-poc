# FieldProof: bounty-first decision and adversarial research

**As of October 2, 2026. Audience: founder and implementation team.**

## Decision

Proceed with the existing physical-contact MVP for Germany MVP, peaq Germany, and Crypto World's Fair.
Freeze its observation type, supply path, payment rail, and receipt format through the MVP deadline.
Research supports technical feasibility and relevant precedents. It does not establish customer demand or venture viability.

The first commercial hypothesis is **permitted operational evidence for a robot already near an external access point**.
The buyer requests a fact from an operator it does not control, checks its provenance and age, and makes an immediate recommendation.
Keep `gate.closed`, `demo-gate`, ESP32-C6, and Solana Devnet x402 for the demonstration.
`DISPATCH` remains a demo recommendation. It does not authorize motion or establish safe passage.

Do not pivot into general robotics training-data collection before these deadlines.
Training datasets and ten-second operational observations serve different jobs.
The former requires capture standards, licensing, task coverage, and quality review that this contact prototype does not provide.

## Corrections to the inherited thesis

1. **Absence in search is not proof of uniqueness.** Replace “absent from all 8,286 projects” with “not identified in inspected records.”
   Semantic retrieval, incomplete evidence, and changing repositories prevent an exhaustive uniqueness claim.
2. **A request object is not a moat.** Specs, freshness windows, priced requests, and demand pooling already appear in related systems.
   A local unmet-demand ledger is easy to copy. Its value requires qualified buyers and permissioned supply.
3. **Winner lift is correlation.** Tags do not establish that Rust, Anchor, IoT, or x402 caused an award.
   Sparse samples, overlapping tags, classification choices, and changing event structures confound the comparison.
4. **Zero winners in a category does not mean zero physical-AI winners.** CrowdBrain won Frontier's grand prize and entered C5.
   Its robotics workflow survives regardless of its category assignment.
5. **Prize status is not financing or revenue.** An unawarded project is not necessarily unfunded, inactive, or commercially unsuccessful.
6. **Crypto is not the only possible payment rail.** Operator accounts, invoicing, subscriptions, and delegated budgets are alternatives.
   Crypto offers a programmable settlement option. Its incremental value requires testing against those alternatives.
7. **Training demand does not validate operational freshness.** A laboratory's dataset budget does not establish demand for `gate.closed`.
8. **Demand aggregation does not automatically fix economics.** Repeated queries can be tests, spam, or self-dealing.
   Counts must not authorize installations or investment without a buyer commitment.

## Price evidence and unit economics

PrismaX publicly lists $9/month for 100 library episodes and $89/month for 1,500 episodes.
At full quota, arithmetic gives $0.09 and approximately $0.0593 per downloaded episode.
These are subscription-quota averages, not marginal sale prices, new-capture costs, or prices for a fresh operational observation.
The published plans delay new releases by 60 or 30 days. Enterprise custom collection is contract-priced.
Access remains limited release. No customer purchase was tested. [Published plans](https://www.prismax.ai/)

Its [customer terms](https://www.prismax.ai/legal) license data for internal use and restrict redistribution and competing data products.
Do not assume buying a dataset grants resale rights.
No inspected primary source establishes a current buyer price for FieldProof's ten-second fact.
The prototype's 0.001 Devnet USDC is an integration test price, not willingness-to-pay or revenue.

Calculate commercial viability per operator site, not by extrapolating a robotics market headline:

```text
net contribution per delivered observation
  = buyer price - provider payout - settlement/transport cost
    - verification cost - expected delivery-failure and recovery cost

monthly site contribution
  = delivered paid observations * net contribution
    - installation amortization - connectivity - support - permission/integration cost

break-even delivered observations
  = monthly site fixed cost / positive net contribution per observation

annual service revenue
  = contracted sites * paid decisions per site per month * retained service price * 12
```

Illustrative sensitivity only: $30 monthly fixed cost and $0.0002 contribution require 150,000 observations per month.
At $0.01 contribution, the same cost requires 3,000. These inputs are assumptions, not estimates.
Measure supplier cost and buyer frequency before setting a production price.
Compare pay-per-query with a prepaid budget or subscription for recurring customers.
Do not build a payment channel before that comparison exposes a material constraint.

No defensible market-size number follows from the inspected evidence.
Build a bottom-up reachable-market estimate from named sites, buyers, useful decisions, and service margin.
Keep hardware sales, training-data budgets, and observation-service revenue separate.

## Try to break the product

| Failure hypothesis | Existing evidence or limit | Smallest useful next test |
|---|---|---|
| The buyer already knows the fact | Nearby robots can sense gates. Owned sites can expose existing controls. | Identify an external operator fact unavailable from the buyer's current system. |
| Freshness expires before action | The recorded signed purchase was accepted at seven seconds within a ten-second window. | Measure purchase-to-use latency and remaining freshness across repeated runs. No service-level claim yet. |
| The signed device lies | P-256 proves key possession and field integrity. Location and wall time remain configured/host-anchored. | Use a known operator, checked installation, and independent physical reference in a pilot. |
| Agreement hides correlated error | Five samples come from one sensor. | Report sample agreement only. Test a faulted sensor before any confidence claim. |
| Payment succeeds but delivery fails | Ledger preserves failed/unknown delivery. Automatic refunds do not exist. | Rehearse recovery and price review cost before a commercial service. |
| Buying data violates operator permissions | Current gateway lacks site-specific access control. | Obtain site permission and define buyer scope before remote exposure. |
| Demand is manufactured | CLI ledger records mock-priced service. Gateway tracks purchase states, including simulations. | Track test traffic separately from independently funded commercial purchases and renewals. |
| Marketplace cold start dominates | No external buyer, remote supplier, or recurring demand exists. | Start with one buyer and one permitted operator. Avoid open discovery and token incentives. |
| Wallet onboarding blocks the customer | Current buyer needs disposable Devnet funding. | Separate judge simulation from integration evidence. Test an operator-funded bounded account for a future pilot. |
| A gate contact gets treated as permission | OPEN does not establish access permission or clear passage. | Keep motion decisions inside the robot's safety controller. Explain the contact stand-in in every demo. |

The [a16z verification research](https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/manipulated-signals-in-depin-protocols) distinguishes signal manipulation and self-dealing.
Its formal guarantees depend on assumptions absent from this single-observer prototype.
[Multicoin's task-market thesis](https://multicoin.capital/2026/03/10/internet-labor-markets/) supports studying request-shaped work and verification.
An investor essay does not validate FieldProof demand.

## Buyer test and research stop rule

The founder selects one reachable robot integrator or facility operator with an immediate cross-organization decision.
No outreach occurred in this work. No customer identity, letter, or willingness-to-pay was invented.

Ask about the last actual incident before presenting the solution:

1. What exact fact was missing, and who controlled it?
2. How did you obtain it, how long did that take, and what did failure cost?
3. What maximum age makes the fact useful at the moment of action?
4. Can the operator legally and contractually supply it to you?
5. Who approves a pilot budget, and what alternative will that budget replace?
6. Will you provide one site, one workflow, a useful-time requirement, and a paid-pilot budget?

A compliment is not validation. A named workflow and a booked technical test establish stronger interest.
A paid pilot establishes an initial purchase, not repeatability. Renewal and full service cost determine the next stage.
Three relevant conversations with no external information gap weaken this wedge.
That is an operating threshold, not statistical proof. Check recruitment quality before abandoning the idea.

Stop broad research now. Reopen only for a buyer fact, eligibility change, competing implementation that changes differentiation, or demonstrated technical blocker.
